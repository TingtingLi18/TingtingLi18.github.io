Research

Science education, educational psychology, and human-centered AI

My research sits at the intersection of science education, educational psychology, learning sciences, and human-centered AI. I examine how humans and generative AI interact, reason, learn, and make decisions across K–16 educational settings, with the goal of informing systems and learning environments that augment human expertise while preserving teacher and learner agency.

Human–AI collaboration

I investigate how teachers, students, and AI systems distribute judgment, evidence use, and intellectual work, and how collaboration structures shape learning and decision making.

Science assessment & feedback

I design and study NGSS-aligned assessment and feedback systems that support knowledge-in-use, formative decision making, and responsive instruction.

Teacher professional judgment

I study how teachers interpret student and classroom evidence, navigate uncertainty, and make instructional decisions with and without AI support.

Equity, agency & sensemaking

I examine how learning environments and AI systems can broaden meaningful participation while keeping key intellectual work and decision authority with teachers and learners.

Interdisciplinary foundations

My academic training includes a Ph.D. in Educational Psychology and Educational Technology, a Ph.D. in Curriculum and Instruction with a focus on science education, an M.Ed. in Curriculum and Instruction in Chemistry, and a B.S. in Chemistry. This interdisciplinary preparation shapes how I connect theories of learning, cognition, motivation, and judgment with science teaching, curriculum and assessment design, and emerging AI technologies.

Methods and design orientation

Across projects, I use mixed methods, design-based approaches, classroom studies, learning analytics, and AI/ML methods to connect system design with evidence about interaction, learning, and educational decision making.
