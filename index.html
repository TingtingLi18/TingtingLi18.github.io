Boeing Distinguished Professor in STEM Education
Assistant Professor of Science Education
Washington State University

I study how humans and generative AI interact, reason, and make decisions across K–16 educational settings. My research uses these interaction mechanisms to inform the design of human-centered AI systems for teaching and learning, with particular attention to teacher professional judgment, student sensemaking, responsive assessment and feedback, and equitable human–AI collaboration.

Research program

Science education, educational psychology, learning sciences, and human-centered AI

My interdisciplinary research draws on science education, educational psychology, learning sciences, and AI to study how people learn, reason, and make decisions with emerging technologies.

01

Human–AI Collaboration

How people and AI share judgment, evidence use, and intellectual work in science teaching and learning.

02

Science Assessment & Feedback

Responsive, NGSS-aligned assessment systems that make student thinking visible and support instructional decisions.

03

Teacher Learning & Judgment

How teachers interpret evidence, navigate uncertainty, and make consequential instructional decisions.

04

Equity & Sensemaking

Learning environments and AI systems that expand meaningful participation and preserve learner agency.

Interdisciplinary training

My academic training spans educational psychology and educational technology, science education and curriculum & instruction, and chemistry. This background informs how I connect theories of learning and cognition with classroom practice, assessment design, and human-centered AI.
